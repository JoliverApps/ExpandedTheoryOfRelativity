Abstract

We develop a framework based on maximal spatiotemporal symmetry in which time is promoted to an on-shell three-dimensional vector subspace. Beginning from a general basis-independent pp-temporal plus qq-spatial linear transformation, the Principle of Relativity together with vectorial time selects a Lorentzian invariant for physically admissible transformations in the full (p+q)(p+q)-dimensional spacetime. Compatibility of temporal-frame transport with this invariant fixes the direction of causal temporal translation, deriving the arrow of time from preservation of the relativistic interval and excluding the usual causal pathologies of multi-time theories. The temporal subspace nevertheless retains a residual rotation within the stabilizer of the causal direction. Relating this transverse rotation to four-dimensional translational action recovers the Planck–de Broglie relations, while its anti-Hermitian generator produces intrinsic spin and admits Abelian and non-Abelian field realizations. The associated angular-velocity relation yields a covariant field-transport equation containing a propagation-orthogonal geometric-source absent from the standard Dirac equation. In the geometric-source-free sector, the spinor realization recovers Dirac dynamics, and the quadratic reduction yields the Klein–Gordon mass shell and Pauli spin–field coupling. Admissible geometric-source structures generate mass, scalar Yukawa, charged-pion isospin, and anomalous magnetic-moment interactions. The resulting theory accommodates U⁢(1)\mathrm{U}(1), SU⁢(2)\mathrm{SU}(2), and SU⁢(3)\mathrm{SU}(3) sectors while preserving a positive-definite conserved density, four-dimensional relativistic kinematics, and Lorentzian causal propagation.

I Introduction

Einstein’s theory of relativity unifies space and time, yet it retains a structural asymmetry: space is a three-dimensional vector domain with intrinsic rotational freedom, whereas time enters as a one-dimensional direction. Expressing local displacements within their respective subspaces as 𝐱s=xs⁢𝐞s\mathbf{x}_{s}=x^{s}\mathbf{e}_{s} and 𝐱t=x0⁢𝐞0\mathbf{x}_{t}=x^{0}\mathbf{e}_{0} makes this asymmetry explicit and motivates the fundamental question: can time possess a three-dimensional vector structure without sacrificing the four-dimensional results of relativity or introducing the causal pathologies common to multi-time formulations; and, if so, how do the resulting dynamics connect to established physics?

To address this question, we propose a framework built on the Principle of Relativity and the Principle of Maximal Spatiotemporal Symmetry. The latter posits that the temporal subspace possesses the same three-dimensional vector structure and algebraic status as the spatial subspace by default, unless physical consistency demands otherwise. From these premises, we demonstrate that the resulting framework is consistent and constitutes a single theory unifying special relativity and quantum physics.

Within this construction, temporal-frame compatibility with the relativistic invariant cc confines causal temporal displacement to a single stabilized temporal axis, thereby establishing a rigorous arrow of time. This constraint decomposes the six-dimensional spacetime into complementary 4+24+2 sectors: a four-dimensional translational spacetime that exactly recovers Einsteinian relativity and an orthogonal two-dimensional temporal plane, termed the Plane-of-Now. The rotational structure of this Plane-of-Now gives rise to phase and spin degrees of freedom, Abelian and non-Abelian gauge structures, as well as mass, Yukawa, and isospin interactions, all without requiring additional independent postulates.

Thus, the Principle of Maximal Spatiotemporal Symmetry enlarges the underlying geometry without disturbing its established relativistic content. The resulting framework provides a common geometric foundation for the principal quantum and field-theoretic structures recovered by the theory while remaining exactly consistent with relativity and four-dimensional translational reality.

A Related Approaches in the Literature

Endowing time with additional dimensions is not itself novel [1, 2, 3]. Previous approaches employ temporal-orientation averaging [4], gauge constraints yielding one-time descriptions [5, 6], independent temporal variables and evolution parameters [7, 8, 9, 10], nonlocal constraints on initial data [11], or symmetry breaking that selects one observable time while leaving other temporal directions hidden [12], while geometric interpretations of quantum phase have also been developed within four-dimensional spacetime [13, 14].

The present theory does not use these mechanisms to restrict the temporal geometry. Instead, time remains a three-dimensional on-shell vector subspace with a structure symmetric to that of space. Compatibility with the relativistic invariant follows solely from the Principle of Relativity and the Principle of Maximal Spatiotemporal Symmetry, with equality between the six-dimensional velocity norm and its four-dimensional Lorentzian expression fixing the causal temporal direction. Accordingly, causal translation is confined to one temporal direction while the remaining two remain active as the Plane-of-Now, providing the geometric setting for quantum phenomena without additional causal clocks, compactification, gauge elimination of temporal components, hidden temporal directions, or supplementary geometric or dynamical constraints.

B Scope and Interpretation

Before proceeding, two points must be clarified. First, the purpose of this foundational paper is to answer the question posed above and to establish the viability and internal coherence of the framework; it does not aim to exhaust every solution or experimental consequence. Second, this framework is an independent geometric theory, not a reformulation of standard quantum mechanics. No quantum structures or explicit relations to standard quantum mechanics are assumed a priori. The theory should therefore be evaluated strictly within its own postulates and internal consistency.

VII Discussion

Extending time to a three-dimensional vector subspace allows the Flag-and-Pole framework to recover several foundational structures of relativity and quantum physics from a common temporal geometry. The admissible inertial branch has a pseudo-Euclidean realization with three positive temporal and three negative spatial directions, while the corresponding quadratic displacement norm reduces exactly to the Lorentzian interval. [ℐz,ℐ±]=±ℐ±,[ℐ+,ℐ−]=2⁢ℐz,[\mathcal{I}_{z},\mathcal{I}_{\pm}]=\pm\mathcal{I}_{\pm},\quad[\mathcal{I}_{+}% ,\mathcal{I}_{-}]=2\mathcal{I}_{z}, (B40)

and form a Plane-of-Now 𝔰⁢𝔲⁢(2)\mathfrak{su}(2) ladder algebra. Since the vector-grade source also obeys {U,Rπ}=0\{U,R_{\pi}\}=0, its action exchanges propagation eigenspaces, with this exchange balanced by the derivative terms in propagation-aligned solutions. In this basis, the mixing operator becomes

Rπ=i⁢gπ⁢π+⁢ℐ+−i⁢gπ⁢π−⁢ℐ−,R_{\pi}=ig_{\pi}\pi^{+}\mathcal{I}_{+}-ig_{\pi}\pi^{-}\mathcal{I}_{-}, (B41)

where

ℐ+:Ψ−→Ψ+,ℐ−:Ψ+→Ψ−.\mathcal{I}_{+}:\Psi_{-}\to\Psi_{+},\quad\mathcal{I}_{-}:\Psi_{+}\to\Psi_{-}. (B42)

Under the transformation

Ω⁢(α)=exp⁡(−i⁢α⁢ℐz),\Omega(\alpha)=\exp\left(-i\alpha\mathcal{I}_{z}\right), (B43)

the transition operators transform as

Ω⁢ℐ±⁢Ω−1=e∓i⁢α⁢ℐ±.\Omega\mathcal{I}_{\pm}\Omega^{-1}=e^{\mp i\alpha}\mathcal{I}_{\pm}. (B44)

Covariance of RπR_{\pi} therefore gives

π±→e∓i⁢α⁢π±,\pi^{\pm}\to e^{\mp i\alpha}\pi^{\pm}, (B45)

so the conjugate mixing fields have weights ±1\pm 1 under the U⁢(1)\mathrm{U}(1) subgroup generated by ℐz\mathcal{I}_{z}, while the spinor columns transform as Ψ±→e∓i⁢α/2⁢Ψ±\Psi_{\pm}\to e^{\mp i\alpha/2}\Psi_{\pm} and have weights ±12\pm\frac{1}{2}. Under local changes of this orientation reference, the Principle of Relativity carries these weights into the covariant derivatives,

Dμ⁢π±=(∂μ±iℏ⁢qI⁢BμI)⁢π±,D_{\mu}\pi^{\pm}=\left(\partial_{\mu}\pm\frac{i}{\hbar}q_{\mathrm{I}}B_{\mu}^{% \mathrm{I}}\right)\pi^{\pm}, (B46)

with the corresponding spinor derivatives

Dμ⁢Ψ±=(∂μ±i⁢qI2⁢ℏ⁢BμI)⁢Ψ±.D_{\mu}\Psi_{\pm}=\left(\partial_{\mu}\pm\frac{iq_{\mathrm{I}}}{2\hbar}B_{\mu}% ^{\mathrm{I}}\right)\Psi_{\pm}. (B47)

Thus, the mixing-field charge is the difference between the two spinor charges, giving ±2⁢q\pm 2q when the spinor charges are ±q\pm q. [ℐz,Rπ]=i⁢gπ⁢π+⁢ℐ++i⁢gπ⁢π−⁢ℐ−≠0,[\mathcal{I}_{z},R_{\pi}]=ig_{\pi}\pi^{+}\mathcal{I}_{+}+ig_{\pi}\pi^{-}% \mathcal{I}_{-}\neq 0, (B48)

so the isospin current

satisfies

∂μJIμ=−i⁢χ⁢Ψ¯⁢[ℐz,Rπ]⁢Ψ.\partial_{\mu}J_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mu}=-i\chi\bar{\Psi}[\mathcal{I}_{z},R_{\pi}]\Psi. (B50)

Consequently, the mixing field carries the charge exchanged between the two spinor sectors.

Completing the charged-field dynamics with

ℒπ=Dμ⁢π−⁢Dμ⁢π+−𝒱π⁢(π−⁢π+)\mathcal{L}_{\pi}=D_{\mu}\pi^{-}D^{\mu}\pi^{+}-\mathcal{V}_{\pi}\left(\pi^{-}% \pi^{+}\right) (B51)

Its field equations give the complementary exchange balance

∂μjπμ=i⁢χ⁢Ψ¯⁢[ℐz,Rπ]⁢Ψ,\partial_{\mu}j_{\pi}^{\mu}=i\chi\bar{\Psi}[\mathcal{I}_{z},R_{\pi}]\Psi, (B53)

so adding the two balances gives

∂μ(JIμ+jπμ)=0.\partial_{\mu}\left(J_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mu}+j_{\pi}^{\mu}\right)=0. (B54)

Together, these relations give the Plane-of-Now vector solution a pion-type charged-pseudoscalar interaction, with isospin raising and lowering operators that exchange the two spinor sectors and a conserved total orientation charge.

F The Interacting Field Lagrangian

Including the scalar mass contribution within 𝐑\mathbf{R}, the complete PO-source is

𝐑total≡𝐑=𝐑PoN+𝐑B+𝐑Other,\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{total}}\equiv\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{PoN}}+\mathbf{% R}_{\mathrm{B}}+\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{Other}}, (B55)

where 𝐑B=𝐢⁢q⁢a⁢(|B|2)⁢𝐁\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{B}}=\mathbf{i}qa\left(|B|^{2}\right)\mathbf{B} is the propagation-orthogonal field-strength solution derived previously, with ρ⁢(𝐑B)=RB=i⁢q⁢a⁢Σz⁢B\rho(\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{B}})=R_{\mathrm{B}}=iqa\Sigma_{z}B, while 𝐑Other\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{Other}} contains the remaining admissible components of the general six-dimensional algebra established in Appendix A.

Combining these contributions gives the represented PO-source operator

R=m⁢c⁢I8+y⁢ϕ⁢Σz+i⁢gπ⁢(π+⁢ℐ+−π−⁢ℐ−)+i⁢q⁢a⁢Σz⁢B+ρ⁢(𝐑Other).\begin{split}R={}&mcI_{8}+y\phi\Sigma_{z}+ig_{\pi}\left(\pi^{+}\mathcal{I}_{+}% -\pi^{-}\mathcal{I}_{-}\right)\\ &+iqa\Sigma_{z}B+\rho\left(\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{Other}}\right).\end{split} (B56)

As established in Section 2, local reference covariance follows from the Principle of Relativity, so the derivative enters as Dμ≔∂μ+iℏ⁢∑aqa⁢Aμa⁢TaD_{\mu}\coloneqq\partial_{\mu}+\frac{i}{\hbar}\sum_{a}q_{a}A_{\mu}^{a}T_{a}, using Hermitian interaction generators TaT_{a} in this convention. Consequently, the kinetic-derivative term provides the corresponding minimal gauge interactions.

Collecting the kinetic, source, and field contributions gives the full interacting Lagrangian,

ℒ=i2⁢Ψ¯⁢Γμ⁢D↔μ⁢Ψ−χ⁢Ψ¯⁢R⁢Ψ+ℒF+ℒB+ℒϕ+ℒπ+ℒROther.\begin{split}\mathcal{L}={}&\frac{i}{2}\bar{\Psi}\Gamma^{\mu}% \overleftrightarrow{D}_{\mu}\Psi-\chi\bar{\Psi}R\Psi\\ &+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{F}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{B}}+\mathcal{L}_{\phi}+% \mathcal{L}_{\pi}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{Other}}.\end{split} (B57)

Here the covariant derivative carries the gauge interactions of the chosen field representation, with generators commuting with the kinetic matrices and sources transforming as R′=g⁢R⁢g−1R^{\prime}=gRg^{-1}. Through its admissible components, the PO-source supplies the spinor mass, scalar Yukawa, charged-pseudoscalar mixing, propagation-orthogonal field-strength, and remaining algebraic interactions, while the accompanying dynamical terms govern the source fields. Specifying the representations, fields, potentials, and coupling constants then selects a particular physical realization.

Finally, the propagation-orthogonal field-strength coupling −χ⁢Ψ¯⁢RB⁢Ψ-\chi\bar{\Psi}R_{\mathrm{B}}\Psi is conventionally classified as a nonminimal electromagnetic field-strength interaction. In the present theory, however, such terms belong to the minimal interaction structure, arising directly from the geometric-source equation alongside the usual minimal electromagnetic coupling. Accordingly, anomalous magnetic responses and nonlinear electromagnetic couplings emerge as expected solutions of the theory and not as ad hoc additions.
